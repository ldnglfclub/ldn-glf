Our Code

What we
stand for.

Joining the club means signing onto something. These aren't suggestions — they're the rules of being here.

"Golf taught us patience, focus, honesty, and how to lose well. We owe the game back by being the kind of players, friends, and people that make it better than we found it."

✦ The Six Rules ✦

The non-negotiables.

01

No racism. Ever.

Golf has a long history of excluding people based on race. We're the opposite. If you make a racist comment, joke, or post — on the course, on the app, in the group chat — your membership ends. No warnings. No second chances.

02

Equality is the standard.

Anyone can play this game. We support golfers regardless of gender, sexuality, religion, ability, or background. Our junior coaching, our drops, our app, our content — everything is built with that in mind.

03

Bullying is the worst miss.

Tearing down your mate over a bad shot, hyping a pile-on, calling someone out for their swing — none of that lives here. If you see it, call it out. If you do it, take time off the club. We have each other's backs.

04

Mental health matters.

The course is a place to clear your head, not lose it. If you're struggling — with school, friends, family, anything — talking to someone is a strength. The club isn't a therapist, but we'll always point you to people who can help. (Childline 0800 1111 if you need someone right now.)

05

Respect the course, the staff, the game.

Repair your pitch marks. Rake the bunker. Don't be rude to the starter. Don't slam clubs. Don't ghost a tee time. The people who run these places work hard so we get to play. Treat them right.

06

Be a good ambassador.

When you're wearing the kit, you're representing the club. When you're in the app talking to other members, you're representing the club. When other young golfers see you — at the range, on the course, online — what they see should make them want in. Carry that weight well.

Course etiquette,
fast version.

DO · Pace of play

Keep up with the group in front. If you're holding people up, wave them through. Ready golf — hit when you're ready, not strictly furthest from the hole.

DON'T · Slow play

Don't take three practice swings on every shot. Don't ride out 80-yard chip walks. Don't make the group behind wait.

DO · Repair the course

Fix your pitch marks on the green. Rake the bunker after you. Replace divots on the fairway or sand them in.

DON'T · Talk during swings

When someone's over the ball, stay still and silent. No phones ringing, no chat. Wait until contact's made.

DO · Phones on silent

You can use it for GPS, score, photos. Silent ringer, no speaker music — unless everyone's into it and there's nobody else around.

DON'T · Throw clubs

Bad shot? Tough. Throwing or slamming a club is the most amateur move there is, gets you removed from clubs, and ruins it for everyone watching.

DO · Call your own penalties

Golf is the only sport where you call your own fouls. Hit it in the trees? Take the drop properly. Whiffed a swing? Count it. Honesty is the whole game.

DON'T · Cheat on the scorecard

Adding a mulligan when nobody's looking, fudging the count — there's no point. The number is for you. Get it right.

The pledge.

"I join the LDN GLF Club not just as a customer, but as part of a community. I'll respect the game, the people around me, and the standards we've agreed on together. I'll stand up for my mates, call out behaviour that doesn't belong, and represent this club like I mean it. Because we don't just play — we set the tone."

— Signed by every member, on the way in.
